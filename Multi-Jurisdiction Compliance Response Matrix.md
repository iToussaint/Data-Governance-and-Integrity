# Multi-Jurisdiction Compliance Response Matrix

**Company:** ShopRwanda

**Scenario:** Global customer data deletion requests

**Regulations:**

- Rwanda: Law No. 058/2021 of 13/10/2021 relating to the Protection of Personal Data and Privacy
- EU: General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)
- California: CCPA as amended by CPRA

---

## 1. Customer A – Toussaint Iradukunda (Rwanda)

### Legal Rights Analysis

- Toussaint has the **right to request erasure** of his personal data, in writing or electronically (Law 058/2021, **Article 23**).
- The right applies where:
  1. the data is no longer necessary for the purpose it was collected for;
  2. he withdraws consent and there is no other legal ground for processing;
  3. he objects and there are no overriding legitimate grounds;
  4. the data was processed unlawfully.
- The right is **not absolute**. Erasure does not apply where processing is necessary (Article 23):
  - for reasons of public interest;
  - for historical, scientific research or statistical purposes;
  - to comply with a legal obligation the company is subject to;
  - to establish, exercise or defend legal claims.

### Company Obligations

- Assess the request against the grounds and exceptions in Article 23
- Delete personal data where no exception applies
- If the data was disclosed to a third party or made public, **inform those third parties** in writing or electronically that erasure was requested (Article 23)
- Inform Toussaint in writing or electronically of the erasure (Article 23)

### Data Retention

- **Rule:** Personal data is kept only until the purposes of processing are fulfilled (**Article 52**).
- **Can retain longer** where (Article 52):
  - retention is authorised by law (e.g., transaction and tax records);
  - retention is required by a contract between the parties;
  - it is needed to enforce a court order or conduct court proceedings.

### Response Deadline

- **30 days** from the date the request is received (Article 23)
- If Toussaint is not satisfied, he may **appeal to the supervisory authority (NCSA) within 30 days** of receiving the response; the NCSA must respond within **60 days** (Article 23)

### Penalties for Non-Compliance

- **Administrative fines (Article 53):** RWF 2,000,000 – 5,000,000 or 1% of global turnover; for a corporate body, **1% of the previous financial year's global turnover**. Covers failures such as operating without a registration certificate, not designating a data protection officer, and breach-notification failures.
- **Criminal penalties:** using or disclosing personal data contrary to the law carries 1–3 years' imprisonment and/or RWF 7,000,000 – 10,000,000 (Article 56). For a corporate body, the fine is **5% of the previous financial year's annual turnover** (Article 62).
- **Additional penalties (Article 63):** confiscation of items and proceeds, and temporary or permanent closure of the business.

### Action Steps

1. Verify Toussaint's identity
2. Check whether any Article 23 exception or Article 52 retention ground applies
3. Delete personal/profile data across all systems
4. Retain only records the law or a contract requires
5. Notify any third parties the data was shared with
6. Inform Toussaint of the outcome within 30 days
7. Log the request and actions taken for audit purposes

### Draft Response

> "Dear Toussaint, we have received and processed your request under Article 23 of Law No. 058/2021. Your personal account data has been erased, and we have informed third parties we shared it with. We have retained only the records we are legally required to keep, and only for as long as the law requires. If you are not satisfied with this response, you may appeal to the National Cyber Security Authority (NCSA) within 30 days."

## 2. Customer B – Lukas (Germany – GDPR)

### Legal Rights Analysis

- Lukas has a **strong right to erasure (Article 17 – "Right to be Forgotten")**

### Exemptions (Article 17(3))

- Data may be retained where processing is necessary for:
  - compliance with a legal obligation (e.g., tax and accounting records)
  - the establishment, exercise or defence of legal claims
  - other listed grounds (freedom of expression, public health, archiving/research in the public interest)

### Company Obligations

- Delete personal data **without undue delay** (Article 17)
- Notify each recipient the data was disclosed to of the erasure, unless impossible or disproportionate (Article 19)
- Ensure full compliance with GDPR

### Response Deadline

- **1 month** from receipt of the request (Article 12(3))
- Can be **extended by two further months** where requests are complex or numerous; Lukas must be informed of the extension and the reasons within the first month

### Penalties for Non-Compliance

- Fines up to **€20 million or 4% of total worldwide annual turnover**, whichever is higher (Article 83(5))

### Action Steps

1. Verify identity
2. Delete personal data across all systems
3. Notify third-party processors and recipients
4. Confirm deletion to user within one month

### Draft Response

> "Dear Lukas, we confirm that your personal data has been erased in accordance with GDPR Article 17. Any required legal records have been securely retained as permitted by law."

## 3. Customer C – Maria (California – CCPA/CPRA)

### Legal Rights Analysis

- Maria has:
  - **Right to deletion** (Cal. Civ. Code §1798.105)
  - **Right to opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information** (§1798.120)

### Can Deletion Be Immediate?

- **No**, due to the active return dispute
- The CCPA allows a business to keep data needed to complete the transaction or fulfil the contract with the consumer (§1798.105(d))

### Company Obligations

- Pause deletion of data needed for the dispute until it is resolved
- Honour the opt-out of sale/sharing **as soon as feasibly possible, and no later than 15 business days** (CCPA Regulations §7026)
- Explain to Maria why part of her deletion request is delayed

### Response Deadline

- **45 calendar days** from receipt of the request
- Can be **extended once by an additional 45 days** (90 days total) if Maria is notified within the first 45 days

### Required Disclosures

- What data is collected
- How it is used/shared
- The legal basis for retaining data during the dispute
- Confirmation of opt-out implementation

### Penalties for Non-Compliance

- Administrative fines of up to **$2,663 per violation** or **$7,988 per intentional violation** or violation involving a minor (§1798.155, amounts adjusted for inflation from January 2025)

### Action Steps

1. Verify identity
2. Stop selling/sharing personal data within 15 business days
3. Inform Maria deletion will occur after dispute resolution
4. Complete deletion once the obligation ends

### Draft Response

> "Dear Maria, we have processed your request to stop the sale and sharing of your personal information. Your deletion request will be completed once your active return dispute is resolved, as permitted under applicable law."

## 5. Key Insights

- **Rwanda** sets a **fixed 30-day deadline with no extension**, and its data protection law itself sets **criminal penalties, including imprisonment** (Arts. 56–62); GDPR and CCPA rely mainly on administrative fines
- **GDPR** carries the **highest administrative fines** and a flexible deadline for complex requests
- **CCPA/CPRA** focuses on **consumer control and opt-out rights**, with the longest response window

## Conclusion

ShopRwanda must handle each request based on **jurisdiction-specific laws**, balancing user rights with legal obligations. Proper identity verification, controlled data deletion, third-party notification and clear communication within each deadline are essential to maintaining compliance and trust.
